"Forced on Israel"

On the morning after the attack, one anonymous leak reached every newsroom. What each desk did with a single verb is the whole story.

Overnight between September 17 and 18, the US outlet Al-Monitor published an intelligence leak: Israel had not planned to blow up the pagers that day. Two Hezbollah operatives had grown suspicious of the devices, and the detonation was triggered early. The decision, in the phrase every copy kept, came "at the 90th minute."

The leak carried a claim of fact, that the timing was unplanned, and an invitation: to say who did what to whom. Here is what happened to it, hour by hour.

September 18

05:28 UTC · Ynet · Israel

"Report: Hezbollah operatives grew suspicious — and the pager explosion 'was forced' on Israel."

דיווח: פעילי חיזבאללה חשדו — ופיצוץ הביפרים "נכפה" על ישראל

Ynet ran the leak twice within four minutes, flash and article, both under its standard "Report:" hedge prefix, both with the same construction: the explosion was forced on Israel. Israel appears in its own attack as the grammatical object, almost the victim of the timing. The scare quotes around "forced" are Ynet's only distancing from the frame it chose.

08:02 UTC · Kikar HaShabbat · Israel

"Hezbollah operatives grew suspicious of the pagers — and Israel decided to detonate them 'at the 90th minute.'"

פעילי חיזבאללה חשדו בביפרים — וישראל החליטה לפוצץ אותם "בדקה ה-90"

Two and a half hours later, the haredi outlet Kikar HaShabbat published the identical facts with the verb turned around: Israel decided to detonate them. No "Report:" prefix, Israel as subject, the decision as a decision. It is the only Israeli headline in the first two days that does this without a hedge. The same article quotes Hezbollah's paper Al-Akhbar calling the operation "murder without rules," and relays journalist Barak Ravid's version of the same leak from a US official: Israel was at the point where "either it uses the booby-trapped pagers or it loses them."

September 18 · Al Jadeed · Lebanon

"Media: operatives discovered the pager breach — and Israel detonated them preemptively!"

إعلام: عناصر اكتشفت خرق البيجر.. وإسرائيل فجرتها استباقياً!

The Lebanese copy of the same leak has no trouble with the verb. Israel detonated. Its body goes further than any Israeli version: "according to intelligence sources relayed by Al-Monitor, the Israeli enemy took an urgent decision…" A Beirut commercial channel, quoting an American site, quoting Israeli intelligence sources, about why Israel bombed Beirut early.

September 18 · Tagesschau · Germany

"Was the operation about to be exposed?" ("Drohte die Aktion aufzufliegen?")

September 18 · Washington Post · US

"A key question behind Israeli attack on Hezbollah devices: Why now?"

The distant desks converted the leak into a question. The Post's analysis piece states the logic the leak implies, that the capability "must be used before it could be discovered and the pagers disarmed, as the newsletter Al Monitor reported," and quotes an expert: "The level of suspicion is high, and it takes only one to explode [unintentionally] for the operation to be burned." Nobody outside Israel needed the passive voice; nobody outside Israel and Lebanon needed a verdict either.

The wink

15:13 UTC · Ynet · Israel

"Karhi published a Bible verse hinting at responsibility for the device explosions."

The same afternoon, while the news desks kept Israel out of the subject position, communications minister Shlomo Karhi tweeted Nahum 2:11, "She is empty, and void, and waste; and the heart melteth, and the knees smite together…", the prophet's taunt over the destruction of Nineveh. Ynet reported the tweet as "hinting at taking responsibility." The official line held in the headlines and cracked in scripture.

The premise dissolves

September 20 · Reuters (exclusive) · carried in the US corpus by Reuters and Yahoo

"Hezbollah handed out pagers hours before blasts — even after checking devices."

Two days later, Reuters reported the opposite premise: there had been no specific suspicion of the pagers at all. Hezbollah's checks were part of a routine sweep of its equipment, and the organization was still distributing pagers hours before they exploded. The leak's founding fact, two suspicious operatives, a burning fuse, a forced hand, was never confirmed. What survived of it was grammar: in Hebrew, an operation Israel had to do; in Arabic, a thing the enemy did; in German and English, a question.

Ynet's "Report:" prefix is a system, not a one-off: 22 of its 95 headlines in the corpus begin with it. Kikar had already dropped the hedge the previous evening. Its 19:17 headline on September 17 read "Drone footage: the Mossad seized the pager shipment and booby-trapped them," while Ynet's Mossad headline the same hour still opened with "Reports:".
